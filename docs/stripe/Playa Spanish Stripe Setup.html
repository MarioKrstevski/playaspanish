Step 1 · before Stripe

Your RFC and your contador

This is the one step that can stop you. In Mexico, Stripe signs up a single person as Persona Física con Actividad Empresarial (sole proprietor). There is no plain "individual" option. So your RFC must be registered with the SAT for self-employed income, not only as an employee. Your name in Stripe must match your RFC exactly.

Ask your contador to confirm your RFC is set up for this, and which regime fits you best (many teachers use RESICO or Actividad Empresarial y Profesional). Bring these questions:

  1. Is my RFC registered for self-employed income, so I can sign up to Stripe as Persona Física con Actividad Empresarial? If not, can we update it?
  2. Which regime is best for me: RESICO or Actividad Empresarial y Profesional?
  3. My students live outside Mexico and pay in US dollars. How is that taxed? Does IVA apply?
  4. Do I need to issue a factura (CFDI) for each payment? If the student is abroad, do I use the generic foreign RFC?
  5. I will pay another teacher from this money. What does he need to give me (a factura?) so I am not taxed on money that goes to him?
  6. Stripe charges IVA on its fees and gives me invoices for them. Can I deduct those?

Step 3 · about 30 minutes

Create the account

You can switch the Stripe dashboard to Spanish at any time from your profile settings. The labels below are in English.

  1. Go to dashboard.stripe.com/registerChoose Mexico as the country. Sign up with your email, your full name and a strong password. Confirm the email Stripe sends you.
  2. Turn on two-step loginStripe will ask for it. Use an authenticator app or SMS. This protects the money, so do not skip it.
  3. Click "Activate payments"This opens the form that lets you receive real money. Until you finish it, you can only use test mode.
  4. Type of businessChoose Sole proprietorship (Persona Física con Actividad Empresarial). Leave the company RFC empty; your personal RFC goes in the personal section. Putting your personal RFC in the company box causes an error.
  5. Your personal detailsLegal name exactly as on your RFC, personal RFC, date of birth, address and phone.
  6. Business detailsIndustry: Education (tutoring or educational services). Website: https://playaspanish.com. For the description, you can paste this:

    Online one-on-one Spanish lessons with native Mexican teachers over Zoom or Teams. Students pay per lesson (USD 25, about 45 minutes of instruction) or buy a pack of 10 lessons in advance (USD 240).

  7. Statement descriptorThis is what students see on their card statement. Use PLAYA SPANISH, so nobody disputes a charge they do not recognize.
  8. Bank accountEnter your 18-digit CLABE. Check it twice; money sent to a wrong CLABE is slow to recover.
  9. Submit and waitStripe usually reviews in a few days. It may email you asking for a photo of your INE or a proof of address. Answer quickly, since payouts stay on hold until they are satisfied.
While you waitEverything in Step 4 works in test mode already. You can build the links now and they will be ready the day the account is approved.
Step 4 · 15 minutes, once

Make your payment links

A payment link is a web address you send by email or WhatsApp. The student opens it, pays by card, and gets a receipt. They do not need an account. You only need two links. Make each link once and reuse it; you do not need a new link for every class.

The website never sends anyone to Stripe directly. Every student writes to you first. After your first conversation, you reply with the link that fits what they chose.

LinkPriceWhen to send it
One lesson (pay as you go)$25 USDFor each class, to students who pay one lesson at a time
10-lesson pack$240 USDOnce, when a student chooses the pack ($24 a lesson, $10 off). Includes the 50% money-back promise.

These are the prices on the website today. If they change, edit the price in the link or make a new link.

  1. Open Payment LinksIn the Stripe dashboard, search for Payment Links and click + New.
  2. Add the productName it the way students should see it, for example Spanish lesson with Playa Spanish. Set the price to 25.00, the currency to USD, and choose One time.
  3. Collect the student's name and emailTurn on the options to collect them, so every payment in your list has a name next to it.
  4. After paymentChoose to show a confirmation message, for example ¡Gracias! See you in class.
  5. Create the link and save itCopy the link and keep it somewhere easy, like a pinned WhatsApp note. Then make the second link the same way: name it 10 Spanish lessons with Playa Spanish, price 240.00 USD.
  6. Watch payments arriveThe Payments page shows who paid and when. The Stripe phone app sends you a notification for each one.
Tip: packs cost you less in feesStripe charges a small fixed amount on every payment, so one $240 pack payment costs less in fees than ten separate $25 payments.
Good to know

What Stripe keeps

Stripe Mexico charges 3.6% + MX$3 per card payment, plus 0.5% for foreign cards and 2% when it converts dollars to pesos. IVA (16%) is added on top of Stripe's fee. For students in the US paying in dollars, that is about 7–8% of each payment. Try your own numbers:

Good to know

Refunds and the 50% promise

The website promises half the money back on the 10-lesson pack if it is not for the student. To do that, open the payment in Stripe, click Refund, and enter half the amount ($120 of a $240 pack). The money goes back to the student's card in about 5–10 days.

Stripe keeps its original fee on refundsIf you refund half of a $240 pack, you still paid the fee on the full $240. Worth knowing, but not a reason to change the promise.

If a student ever disputes a charge with their bank, Stripe charges MX$150 and emails you. Reply with the class dates; that is usually enough.

Good to know

Paying the other teachers

All student payments go to you, into your own account. Stripe does not split money between teachers. When you pay Neptalí or any other teacher, do it from your bank as a normal transfer.

For taxes, the SAT sees all of it as your income unless you can show what you paid out. Keep a simple list of which classes each teacher gave and what you paid them. Ask your contador (question 5 in Step 1) what the teachers should give you so those payments count as your expense.

Stripe's fees, requirements and payout times are from its public pages as of November 2026 and can change. This guide explains the setup; for taxes, your contador has the final word.
